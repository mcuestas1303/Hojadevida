```text
Remember rewinding a cassette with a pencil? Tin and Tan work out how many turns it really takes.
A nostalgia check for anyone who grew up with cassettes in the 1970s, 80s and 90s.

The math, step by step:
- A C90 tape plays 45 minutes per side, which is 2,700 seconds.
- Tape speed: 1 7/8 inches per second (4.76 cm/s), the standard speed of the compact cassette.
- 2,700 s x 1 7/8 in/s = about 5,060 inches, about 422 feet of tape (128.5 m).
- One pencil turn winds about 4 1/3 inches (11 cm; pi x 1.4 in).
- 5,060 inches divided by 4 1/3 inches is about 1,170 turns.
- At 2 turns per second: about 585 seconds, almost 10 minutes. For one side.

This is an estimate. It assumes an average spool diameter of 1.4 in (3.5 cm), which grows as the tape winds. Tape speed: 1 7/8 in/s (4.76 cm/s). Real tapes and real hands vary.

Source for the tape speed: Philips Technical Review, vol. 31, 1970: "The design of the Compact Cassette is based on a tape speed of 4.76 cm/s."
https://www.musicaememoria.com/docs/PTechReview-31-1970-077.pdf
```
